---
title: Rate limits
description: Counted per key rather than per account, and reported on every response.
---

Allowances are counted **per key**, not per account. A noisy integration cannot
spend the allowance its owner needs to use the website, and one runaway script
cannot lock somebody out of their own account.

<LimitTable />

## Headers

Every response carries the numbers, including the ones that are allowed, so a
client can slow down before it hits the wall rather than after.

```http
X-RateLimit-Limit: 600
X-RateLimit-Remaining: 597
X-RateLimit-Reset: 1788568502
```

`X-RateLimit-Reset` is a Unix timestamp anchored to the **oldest call in the
window**, not to now. It does not move away as you keep trying, so a client can
wait for it once instead of backing off blindly.

## Over the ceiling

```json
{
  "error": {
    "code": "rate_limited",
    "message": "This key has used its allowance for now.",
    "retry_after": 118
  }
}
```

## The other ceiling

Publishing a picture is counted twice: once against the key's `write`
allowance, which counts requests, and once against twenty pictures an hour per
account, which counts pictures. The second is the one that binds, because a
picture costs processing and storage rather than a row.
